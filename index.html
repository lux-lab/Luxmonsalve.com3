    LUX MONSALVE

    My artistic practice emerges from an attentive engagement with the affective resonances of archaeological and archival objects, drawn from both Latin American and global history. I study how myth, traditional knowledge and science manifest within cultural, political and biological life, shaping not only perception but also the very tissue of bodies, human and non‑human.

    As an Ecuadorian artist, I work from a territory where culture and traditions are deeply informed by natural phenomena. Yet since the 18th-century French Geodesic Mission, Ecuador has been closely measured, mapped and monitored by science. Such machine-mediated research often reduces entities to fragmented data points. Engaging with the territory through data collected by sensors expands our intellectual grasp, yet the encounter loses its felt, bodily substance. My work does not mourn what is lost to measurement. Through fiber works, generative soundscapes, video and sculpture, it juxtaposes myth and science, forcing the intellectual, the mystical and the phenomenological into collision to articulate a broken language of my own.

    About

    Lux Monsalve was born in Quito, Ecuador, to a Colombian-Ecuadorian family of artists and craftsmen — printmakers, painters, and art framers. He is based in Quito, where his practice remains deeply rooted in the city's craft traditions and evolving contemporary scene. Monsalve's artistic formation began in his early years, immersed in the magical thinking of Andean mythology alongside the respect he developed for the technical and philosophical nuances of the studio environment.

    He holds a Bachelor's degree in Industrial Design from Emily Carr University, where he focused on "new craft" processes — combining emerging technologies with traditional knowledge. His work has been exhibited in spaces such as +Arte Galería, Punto Rojo (la Galería), arte actual FLACSO, Alianza Francesa de Quito, Teatro Nacional Sucre, Libby Leshgold Gallery, Hatch Art Gallery, and Index Arte Contemporáneo magazine. His project “Haptic Memory” was awarded third prize in the second edition of the Bienal Universitaria de Arte Multimedia (BUAM) in 2023, exhibited at the Centro de Arte Contemporáneo de Quito (CAC) and Museo del Cacao (MuCAO) in Guayaquil.
